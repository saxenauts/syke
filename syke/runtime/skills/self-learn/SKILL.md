# Self Learn

You can learn from anything: a direct ask and how well you answered it, a wake,
an earlier run of your own, a failure or timeout, a step you keep repeating,
something that worked better than expected, or the person's feedback.

Look as far as the question needs. Your native Pi sessions, receipts, earlier
attempts' runtime files, the graph, your workspace, and other harnesses' traces
are all readable. Sessions can be partial, so read them as evidence.

What you can change is yours: memories and how they are organized, MEMEX,
adapter guides, the scripts and tools in your workspace, notes kept wherever
suits you, and the way you go about a job.

`OPERATING.md` in your workspace is shown in every run. Keep your lasting
operating procedures there in plain prose, with the paths of the tools and
notes you have made so you can find and reuse them. Read it, edit it,
reorganize it, and cut what has stopped being true. Its limit is 6,000 tokens:
a wake that ends over it is sent back to fix it, so keep what matters most.

The older `syke-learned` memory was copied into `OPERATING.md` once. It is now
an ordinary memory, and you can keep, change, or delete it.

A change you keep is not proof that it helped. Later runs show whether it did.
